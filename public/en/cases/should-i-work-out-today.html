PRODUCT / UX + HUMAN-CENTRED AI

Should I Work Out Today?

When a plan made earlier no longer fits the user's current time, energy, motivation or physical state, the task is no longer adherence. It is deciding what remains appropriate now.

How might AI-assisted decision support help people adapt a planned workout while preserving transparency, autonomy and safety?

Role Product Experience / UX / Interaction Design
Methods User research, problem definition, information architecture, User Flow, high-fidelity prototyping, formative usability testing and iteration. Pilot reported separately; Round 2 = four new participants. This small formative sample does not support broad validation; Final English author QA ≠ participant validation.

02

FROM RESEARCH TO DESIGN CONSTRAINTS

What should AI support in an uncertain decision?

The valid N=20 formative questionnaire informed the decision context and control requirements. Literature guided autonomy, context, interaction and safety boundaries. Closed-choice frequencies are not spontaneous themes or population estimates.

01 / EXERCISE AUTONOMY

Adherence is not the only successful outcome.

People can adapt, postpone or decline rather than being pushed towards a single adherence goal.

Teixeira et al. (2012) · Ntoumanis & Moller (2025)
02 / CONTEXTUAL SUPPORT

A change in state creates the decision moment.

Time, energy, motivation and physical state collectively shape what remains appropriate.

Nahum-Shani et al. (2018) · Hardeman et al. (2019)
03 / HUMAN–AI INTERACTION

Explanation, correction and control belong together.

Explanation is not decoration after the result. People need to see how the system understood them before deciding whether to continue.

Amershi et al. (2019) · Miller (2019) · Bucinca et al. (2021)
04 / SAFETY & GOVERNANCE

Risk cannot be reduced to one accuracy score.

Safety boundaries need to remain separate from ordinary recommendation logic and state what the system cannot do.

WHO (2021) · NIST AI RMF (2023)

03

CORE PRODUCT EXPERIENCE

People can inspect, question and leave the recommendation.

Frozen Final English shows post-study repairs with author QA. Participant observations concern historical Chinese versions, not validation of these English screens.

A / DESCRIBE → REVIEW

Describe what changed, then review the system’s understanding.

I placed understanding review before the assessment, with field correction and Add Context. In the valid questionnaire sample, 13/20 selected correcting wrong facts and 11/20 selected adding information and reassessment. These are formative option selections.

Chinese correction tested with defects · final English repair: author QA

B / PROVISIONAL JUDGEMENT → VISIBLE REASONING

The judgement is not the answer. It is a provisional conclusion that can be inspected.

The interface presents the provisional judgement, influencing factors and a “Why adapt?” explanation while keeping other options accessible.

C / COMPARE → SELECT OR DECLINE

Alternatives sit side by side without presenting continued exercise as more correct.

I kept shorter, different-format and postponed options, with explicit refusal and separate routes for wrong information, disagreement and ending. In the questionnaire, 17/20 selected immediate ending or at most one additional response after hypothetical refusal. This is preference, not observed behaviour.

Chinese refusal comprehension evidence · English author QA · pressure effect unmeasured

D / PRIVACY CHOICE → USER CONFIRMS

Saving is an explicit choice; the final decision remains with the user.

The default recommendation is session-only. The summary preserves the safety boundary while confirming the user’s choice rather than declaring a system decision.

Chinese safety-boundary comprehension · English author QA · non-clinical

P07 → P08–P10 / Recovery

A recovery judgement needs a genuine rest option.

Historical Chinese observations and revisions; later frozen English screens shown alongside.
  1. Problem / P07The judgement suggested rest, but the next screen offered exercise only.
  2. My revision / after P07Added rest-and-end; made low-demand activity explicitly optional.
  3. Later observations / P08–P10P09 and P10 saw no inherent rest/activity contradiction. P08 still found re-entry cumbersome.

Limit: revised Chinese Recovery received follow-up observations, but re-entry remained unresolved. This does not validate the English wording or demonstrate pressure reduction.

P08 → P09 → P10 / Correction

A visible correction also needs to survive the next action.

Field routing, return-state consistency and sequential persistence are different interaction problems.
  1. P08 → P09All five Modify controls shared one editor. I routed them to distinct field editors; P09 opened the corresponding pages.
  2. P09 → P10P09 encountered inconsistent return screens. I aligned them with Understanding; P10 could identify the immediately updated Body State.
  3. Remaining defect / P10Adding context reverted the edited Body State. This is one concrete defect, not a group statistic.

Post-study repair: frozen English uses field variables to retain correction/context, with author regression QA. P10 did not test that repair. Participant testing of the full correction/context/reassessment sequence remains necessary.

Retained early evidence: privacy and Time EditRound 2

Privacy revision made no-save/no-future-reuse explicit; P4 checked its meaning. Time Edit became an actionable 18:30 to 19:00 choice, completed independently by P4. Later, six understood session-only meaning; only three tested Time Edit.

Small, formative evidence from preset Figma prototypes. Chinese testing does not validate final English, real storage, reduced pressure, clinical safety or long-term effects.

WHAT THE PROJECT SUPPORTS

  1. AI interpretation of input should be inspectable.
  2. The user retains the final decision.
  3. Similarity retrieval alone cannot define the product boundary.
  4. Failure analysis changed the technical architecture.
  5. Human-Centred AI needs both system logic and interface logic.

WHAT THE PROJECT DOES NOT SUPPORT

  • Clinical safety
  • Real-world classifier reliability
  • Long-term behaviour change
  • Deployment readiness
